## 5. What to copy, and what not to

**Copy the system:**

1. Bundle ladder with the top saving stated in the headline.
2. Guarantee in nearly every ad.
3. Fixed headlines, high creative volume, bulk launches, fast pruning.
4. Black Friday started early and layered on top of evergreen ads rather than replacing them.
5. Two entry products, each with its own headline.

**Do not copy the claims.** A supplement can make structure/function claims ("supports digestive comfort") with substantiation and the FDA disclaimer. It cannot claim to treat or prevent a disease or condition. On that basis:

- **Avoid entirely:** parasites, inflammation, blood sugar, immune "boosting" against illness, weight loss, "removes toxins". These are the claims that draw FTC and FDA action and Meta rejections, and a competitor running them is not evidence they are safe to run.
- **Usable with care:** occasional bloating, digestive comfort, regularity, daily energy, a daily routine, ingredient story, taste and ease.
- Meta also rejects ads that imply a personal health condition ("Tired of your bloated belly?"). Write about the product and the routine, not the viewer's body.
- Soursop carries a known safety question (annonacin, linked to neurotoxicity at high or prolonged intake). Keep serving guidance conservative and do not suggest "more is better".

## 6. Fix before scaling

Scaling spend onto these three issues multiplies them.

1. **Subscription and support complaints.** Make one-time purchase the default or make the subscription unmistakable at checkout, add self-serve cancellation, and answer support email. The FTC treats hard-to-cancel subscriptions as a violation, and paid traffic will check Trustpilot.
2. **Advertiser identity.** Run from a Nutrively brand page. If Maya Cooper and Melissa Collins are real creators, use Meta's partnership ads so the brand is disclosed. If they are invented personas, stop: that breaks Meta's rules on inauthentic identity and risks the ad account, and testimonials from people who do not exist are deceptive under FTC rules.
3. **Discount honesty.** "Save up to X%" has to be measured against a price genuinely charged, and the top tier has to deliver X.

## 7. The launch plan

### Offer

- Ladder: 1 bottle, 3 bottles, 6 bottles, with the saving rising by tier and free shipping from the middle tier. Headline uses the top-tier figure.
- Money-back guarantee named in every ad. Match or beat the common 30 days only if refunds are actually honored quickly.
- Differentiator against single-ingredient bitters: four ingredients in one dropper (soursop, moringa, sea moss, ashwagandha), ten seconds a day.

### Headlines (hold constant, as they do)

- "Save Up to [X]% on Soursop Green Drops Today"
- "Shop Soursop Green Drops Now"
- "Black Friday Sale Is Live"
- "Try It Risk-Free for [N] Days"

### Creative matrix: angle x format x hook

| Angle | Why | Formats to produce |
|---|---|---|
| Occasional bloating and digestive comfort | Their top theme (70%) | Creator talking to camera; static benefit card |
| Daily energy without another pill | Their second theme (58%) | Morning-routine video; static |
| The 10-second routine | Already in Nutrively's ads; ease of use | Hands-only dropper demo; GIF-style loop |
| Four ingredients in one bottle | Nutrively's differentiator | Ingredient breakdown static; founder explainer |
| Risk reversal | 90% of their ads say guarantee | Static offer card; text-on-video end card |
| Real customer words | Their weakest area (4% mention reviews), so open ground | Review screenshots, with permission |
| Offer and urgency | Black Friday | Bundle-ladder static; countdown variants |

Three hooks per angle per format gives roughly 40 ads for the first batch. That is enough to learn from; 413 is their scale after months, not a starting requirement.

### Account structure

- **Testing campaign:** one ad set per angle, broad US targeting, new batch weekly.
- **Scaling campaign:** Advantage+ sales campaign holding only ads that passed testing.
- **Retargeting:** small, offer-led, for site visitors and video viewers.
- Billing currency should match the US market if the US is the target; the current ads bill in CAD.

### Rules (fill in once the numbers exist)

- Break-even cost per order = average order value x gross margin after shipping and refunds. **MISSING.**
- Pause an ad after it spends about 1.5x the target cost per order with no purchase.
- Move an ad to scaling after it beats target cost per order over at least 3 days.
- Launch a new batch every week; replace the weakest third.

### Calendar

| Dates | Action |
|---|---|
| Nov 19 to 22 | Fix-first items; build offer ladder; produce batch 1; confirm purchase tracking |
| Nov 23 to 26 | Launch evergreen and early-access Black Friday ads |
| Nov 27 to Dec 1 | Black Friday through Cyber Monday: full offer, daily checks, prune fast |
| Dec 2 to 14 | Last-chance and gift angles; batch 2 from winning angles |
| January | "New year, new routine" angle; subscription offer once cancellation is fixed |

The competitor has a three-week head start on Black Friday. Launching evergreen ads this week matters more than a perfect Black Friday creative set.

### What to track

Cost per new customer, blended revenue against total ad spend, refund rate, subscription cancellations in the first 60 days, and Trustpilot score. An ad that sells and then generates refunds and complaints is not a winner.
