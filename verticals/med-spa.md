# Vertical Playbook: Med Spa / Aesthetics

Read with the active client's `product-marketing.md`. Client facts override anything here.

## Economics that drive every decision
- **LTV is the whole game.** Injectables (neurotoxin every ~3-4 months, filler annually-ish) and memberships create recurring revenue; a new injectables patient is worth many times their first visit.
- **Service lines and their roles:**
  - Neurotoxin / filler: core recurring revenue, the main acquisition target
  - Laser hair removal: package sales, strong first-visit revenue, seasonal (start in fall/winter for summer results)
  - Skin treatments (microneedling, chemical peels, facials, IPL/photofacial, RF microneedling): retention and cross-sell
  - Body contouring: high ticket, longer consideration
  - Medical weight loss (GLP-1 programs): high demand, heavily regulated advertising, membership-style revenue
  - Memberships: monthly dues with treatment credits; smooth cash flow and lift visit frequency
- Ask for: provider count and utilization, new-patient show rate, rebook rate, average spend per visit, membership count and churn, top services by margin, booking platform (Boulevard, Zenoti, Mangomint, Vagaro, AestheticsPro).

## Seasonality
- Holiday season (Nov-Dec) and pre-summer (Mar-May) are peaks; wedding season drives skin and injectables.
- Laser hair removal and body contouring: start campaigns in late summer/fall for results by summer.
- January: memberships and "new year" skin plans; post-holiday slowdowns are good for promos to existing patients.

## Channel mix
- **Meta / Instagram:** primary acquisition channel for most med spas. Provider-led video, UGC-style patient stories (with consent), treatment explainers, limited-time promos. Lead forms or booking-link ads; speed-to-lead SMS within minutes is critical.
- **Google Search:** high intent for "botox near me", "laser hair removal near me". Expect policy friction on brand-name prescription drug terms (see compliance).
- **GBP:** primary category "Medical spa"; secondary categories for real services (laser hair removal service, skin care clinic). Photos of the space and providers, reviews mentioning treatments.
- **Influencer / creator partnerships:** local creators getting treatments, with clear #ad disclosure.
- **Email/SMS to the patient base:** rebooking reminders on the tox cycle (~12-16 weeks), birthday offers, membership upsells, new-treatment launches. Usually the highest-ROI channel.
- **Brand loyalty programs:** manufacturer programs (e.g., Allē for Allergan products, Aspire for Galderma) as retention and promo levers.

## Offers that work
- New-patient offers on neurotoxin (per-unit pricing or a dollar-off first treatment)
- Membership with monthly credits and member-only pricing
- Laser hair removal package pricing, "buy X sessions get one"
- Event days (injector events with deposits), referral credits
- Financing (Cherry, PatientFi, CareCredit) for high-ticket treatments

## Compliance (strict; check every asset)
- **Medical claims:** these are medical procedures performed under medical supervision. No guaranteed results, no "risk-free", no "painless" absolutes. Include provider credentials accurately (MD, NP, PA, RN) and follow state medical board advertising rules and supervision disclosure requirements, which vary by state.
- **Trademarks:** brand names (Botox, Juvederm, Dysport, HydraFacial, CoolSculpting, etc.) are trademarks; use them only for the genuine product they actually use, follow manufacturer brand guidelines (capitalization, ® on first use where required), and don't use them generically ("baby botox" with a different product is a problem).
- **Google Ads:** prescription drug terms are restricted in the US; many neurotoxin and GLP-1 ads get limited or disapproved. GLP-1 and online pharmacy advertising generally requires LegitScript certification. Plan generic-language ads ("wrinkle relaxer treatments", "injectables") as the fallback.
- **Meta:** health and wellness policies restrict before/after imagery, content implying negative self-perception ("tired of your wrinkles?"), and targeting based on personal attributes. Weight loss ads face age and imagery restrictions. Build compliant creative first; don't rely on appeals.
- **Compounded GLP-1s:** don't imply FDA approval for compounded drugs or equivalence to brand-name products; FDA and FTC have actively pursued deceptive weight-loss claims.
- **Before/after photos:** written patient consent, no retouching, same lighting/angle, "results vary" disclosure. Check platform rules separately from legal rules.
- **Testimonials and influencers:** FTC Endorsement Guides apply; disclose material connections; no fake or incentivized reviews (FTC rule on fake reviews).
- **HIPAA / privacy:** don't put tracking pixels on authenticated patient portals or pages that capture health information without a BAA-compliant setup; be cautious with retargeting on treatment-specific pages and with sending health data to ad platforms (Meta/Google conversion APIs). Consult the client's privacy counsel for their setup.
- **Pricing ads:** per-unit pricing must match what's charged; disclose minimums.

## CRO notes
- Online booking with deposit reduces no-shows; show next-available times.
- Treatment pages: candidacy, what to expect, downtime, provider bio, pricing range, financing, real reviews. Provider photos outperform stock.
- Speed-to-lead: SMS within 5 minutes for Meta leads; most leads go cold within an hour.

## KPIs
Cost per booked consult, show rate, cost per new patient who received treatment, 90-day rebook rate, membership count and churn, revenue per patient per year, provider utilization.
