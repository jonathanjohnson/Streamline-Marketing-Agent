# Vertical Playbook: HOA / Community Association Management

Read with the active client's `product-marketing.md`. Client facts override anything here.

## This is B2B, not home services
- **The buyer is a volunteer board**, usually 3-7 homeowners, sometimes with a developer still in control (new communities pre-turnover). Decisions are by vote, often at or around a board meeting, and contracts usually renew annually or at fiscal year end.
- **Revenue model:** management fee per door (per unit) per month, plus ancillary fees (resale certificates/estoppels, violations, collections, special projects). Community size matters: a 500-door community is worth far more than a 40-door one, but small communities may be underserved and easier to win.
- **Switching is the core motion.** Most new business is a community leaving its current manager. Pain is the trigger: unanswered emails, financial reporting errors, slow work orders, manager turnover.
- Service tiers: full-service management, financial-only management, developer/new-construction transition, HOA vs condo vs townhome vs master-planned vs commercial associations.
- Ask for: door count under management, ideal community size range, fee per door, close rate on proposals, average contract tenure, manager-to-community ratio.

## Seasonality
- Budget season (often late summer through fall for calendar-year associations) is when boards evaluate costs and vendors. Annual meetings and board elections bring new members who are open to change.
- Fiscal year-end transitions mean outreach needs to land 60-120 days before contract renewal windows.

## Keyword clusters
- **Commercial intent:** hoa management company [city], hoa management companies near me, condo association management, community association management [city], hoa management fees, hoa management cost per unit
- **Switching:** how to change hoa management company, firing hoa management company, hoa management contract termination
- **Self-managed boards (strong segment):** self managed hoa vs management company, hoa board responsibilities, how to run an hoa board meeting, hoa reserve study
- **Developer transition:** hoa turnover from developer, developer transition checklist
- Content wins here: board education (budgets, reserve studies, collections policy, state statute explainers) earns trust, links and AI citations. Homeowner-facing complaint content brings the wrong audience; target board members.

## Channel mix
- **SEO and AI search:** the core channel. Boards research online and ask AI tools "best HOA management companies in [city]". City and community-type landing pages, comparison content, transparent pricing ranges, case studies of switches.
- **Google Search:** narrow, high-CPC, low-volume; tight geo, exact/phrase match on "hoa management company" terms, aggressive negatives (homeowner complaint, jobs, careers, "my hoa").
- **GBP:** primary category "Property management company" or a closer fit if available; reviews from board members (not just homeowners, who skew negative). Monitor and respond to homeowner reviews calmly; boards read them.
- **Industry:** CAI (Community Associations Institute) chapter involvement, sponsorships, education events; referrals from attorneys, CPAs, reserve study firms, insurance brokers, landscapers.
- **Outbound:** board lists from public records (state corporation filings list HOA officers in many states) + direct mail + email. Use `prospecting` + `cold-email`; tone is consultative, not salesy.
- **LinkedIn:** limited for board members; better for developer and builder relationships.
- **Meta:** low priority except retargeting site visitors and lookalikes of board-member lists.

## Offers that work
- Free management assessment / "second opinion" review of current financials and contract
- Transition guarantee: documented 60/90-day onboarding plan with named manager
- Transparent pricing calculator (fee per door ranges by community type)
- Board training workshop or webinar (lead magnet and trust builder)
- Developer transition package for builders

## CRO notes
- Proposal request form asking: community name, type, door count, current management situation, contract end date, decision timeline. This qualifies and routes.
- Proof boards care about: response time SLAs, manager-to-community ratio, financial reporting samples, technology (owner portal, online payments, work order tracking), years in business, CAI credentials (CMCA, AMS, PCAM).

## Compliance and trust
- State CAM licensing applies in some states (e.g., Florida requires licensed community association managers). Use correct credentials and license numbers where required.
- Don't disparage named competitors; boards share proposals and talk to each other.
- Financial claims (savings, collection rates) need documented basis.

## KPIs
Proposal requests, proposals sent, win rate, doors added per quarter, average fee per door, contract retention rate, time from first touch to signed contract (long; often months).
