# Vertical Playbook: Pest Control

Read with the active client's `product-marketing.md`. Client facts override anything here.

## Economics that drive every decision
- **Recurring plans are the business.** Quarterly or bi-monthly general pest plans carry the LTV; one-time treatments are acquisition tools. Value a lead at `initial service + (recurring price x expected renewals)`.
- **Service lines have very different value and urgency:**
  - General pest (recurring, mid ticket)
  - Termite (high ticket: treatment, bait stations, annual renewals; WDO inspections for real estate closings)
  - Rodent (exclusion work is high ticket, strong fall/winter demand)
  - Mosquito and tick (seasonal recurring)
  - Bed bugs (urgent, high ticket, high anxiety, discreet service)
  - Wildlife (often licensed separately; check whether the client does it)
  - Commercial (restaurants, property managers, HOAs; contract-based, B2B funnel)
- Ask for: initial vs recurring price, cancel rate after first service, termite renewal rate, door-to-door sales volume (if they run a summer program, paid media competes with or feeds it).

## Seasonality
- Spring (Mar-May): ant and termite swarm season; swarmer calls are the peak termite lead source.
- Summer: mosquitoes, wasps, general pest peak; door-to-door season for companies that run it.
- Fall (Sep-Nov): rodents and overwintering pests move indoors; rodent exclusion demand climbs.
- Winter: rodents, cockroaches, bed bugs; use for renewals, termite inspections and commercial prospecting.
- Southern markets have flatter curves; northern markets swing hard.

- **Real estate:** WDO inspection, termite letter for closing, wood destroying insect report
- **Commercial:** commercial pest control, restaurant pest control, property management pest control
- Content that earns links and AI citations: pest ID guides with real local photos ("what are these tiny black ants in [state]"), seasonal pest calendars by region.

## Channel mix
- **Google LSA (pest control category):** high-intent, typically one of the strongest sources. Watch for lead quality on bed bug and wildlife calls the client doesn't handle; dispute or exclude services.
- **GBP:** primary category "Pest control service"; consider adding "Termite control service" if applicable. Photos of techs, trucks, actual pests found. Respond to every review. Geo-grids around the service area via GMB Crush; pest is very proximity-sensitive.
- **Google Search:** separate campaigns per service line (termite and rodent exclusion get their own budgets because their value is much higher). Emergency/bed bug terms need call extensions and fast answer times.
- **Meta:** seasonal pushes (mosquito season, fall rodent), neighborhood-radius retargeting, testimonial video from real techs. Lead form with pest type and property type.
- **Realtor partnerships:** WDO inspections funnel termite treatment and new-homeowner recurring plans.
- **Commercial:** prospecting lists of restaurants, property managers, HOAs, schools. Use `prospecting` + `cold-email`.

## Offers that work
- Initial service discounted (or free) with an annual agreement
- Free termite inspection (pair with a swarm-season urgency angle)
- Free re-treatment between visits guarantee (a strong risk reversal; confirm terms)
- Bundles: general pest + mosquito, general pest + termite monitoring
- Bed bug: free inspection, discreet unmarked vehicles (only if true)

## CRO notes
- Pest-specific landing pages convert far better than a generic "services" page. Match ad group to page.
- Show the guarantee, local license number, years in business, review count above the fold.
- Click-to-call dominates on mobile; track calls with duration thresholds and tag by pest type in the CRM.

## Compliance (do not skip)
- **Pesticide safety claims:** EPA rules under FIFRA prohibit safety claims such as "safe", "non-toxic", "harmless", "no risk" for pesticide products, and state regulators apply similar standards to applicator advertising. Avoid "pet safe" and "kid safe" unqualified. Use accurate framing ("we use targeted treatments and follow label directions", "family- and pet-conscious application practices") and have the client confirm.
- "Green", "organic", "natural" claims need substantiation (FTC Green Guides) and can't imply zero risk.
- State licensing: include license numbers where the state requires it in advertising.
- Door-to-door and telemarketing: local solicitation permits, TCPA and DNC rules for outbound calls and texts.

## Retention
- Service notes and photos after each visit, auto-renew reminders, "pest pressure" seasonal emails, re-service requests handled fast (callbacks are the biggest cancel trigger).

## KPIs
Cost per sold recurring plan, first-90-day cancel rate, termite renewal rate, revenue per service line by channel, answer rate on calls, LSA dispute rate.
